<Deck deck="year" />

The California year in five-day windows. Today's window is given, you draw its companion, and what you write beside a window waits there until the year brings it round again. This is the deck for the morning, with the cup.

<h2>Seventy-two windows</h2>

Japan's old calendar divides the year into seventy-two kō of about five days, each named for something happening in nature: east wind melts the ice, first cherry blossoms, rotten grass becomes fireflies. This deck keeps that grid, and the twenty-four solar terms it hangs on, and names each window for California: almond bloom in the valley, May Gray on the coast, hills the color of lions, Santa Anas coming down from the desert, newts walking to the creeks after the first storms. The names are things happening in the state right now, from the coast to the desert, so the deck asks you to go outside and check.

A Mediterranean year does not move through four temperate seasons, so the ring proposes California's own. **Rain** runs from the first storm to the almond bloom. **Bloom** runs through the wildflowers. **Gold** is the dry summer hills. **Ember** is fire weather and harvest.

<h2>How to use it</h2>

Open it in the morning. The world's window is face up on the left, the card the date gives. Turn the needle. It goes round twice and rests on another window: yours. Hold the two cards together before deciding what they mean. Read opens the book: what is happening now and where, the window you drew and its dates, a passage chosen by its season, the old Japanese name for today's window, last year's line for this window if you wrote one, and one question about the distance between them. Then open the notebook and write one line on today's page. What you saw outside counts as much as what you felt.

Every line lands on today's page under its year, so the notebook grows by one line a day and by one year a year. When a window comes round again, its page shows every past year's line first. At the equinoxes and solstices the ring lights the season just ended and the notebook opens its pages for review: the autumn accounting of the soul the [Rituals](rituals.html) page already names, now with something to account.

The needle turns as often as you like. The world's window stays where the date puts it, so the deck can also be opened just to check the time.
