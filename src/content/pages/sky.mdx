<Deck deck="sky" />

The heavens as they stand over you tonight, read the way the ancients read them: as symbol, as a ladder, and as a mirror. You lift your eyes, a plate is drawn by chance, and the sky shows where its light stands tonight. Look past the stars, and a mystery may answer instead. Nothing here predicts.

*That which is below is like that which is above & that which is above is like that which is below to do the miracles of one only thing.* The Emerald Tablet, in Isaac Newton's translation.

<h2>Seven heavens, the fixed stars, and the mysteries</h2>

The seven wandering lights the ancients could see with their eyes are the rungs of every ladder people have built to the sky: Inanna's seven gates, the seven heavens of the night journey, the Hermetic ascent that sets a burden down at each sphere, Dante's Paradiso, the seven days of the week. The fixed stars are the figures every people drew: the twelve signs along the sun's road, from the Ram to the Fishes, and around them the Pleiades, Orion, the Bear, the Pole Star, the Dragon, the Milky Way, the Dog Star, the Swan, the Weaver and the Cowherd, the Queen, the Herdsman, the Chained Woman, the Hero, the Winged Horse, the Charioteer, the Crown, the Kneeler, the Serpent-Bearer, the Crow and the Cup, Berenice's Hair, the River, the Whale, the Dolphin, the Southern Fish, the Little Cloud, and the falling stars of August and December. The mysteries are what you find when you look past the stars: the silence, the old light, the world egg, the great year, the edge, the long sleep, the last light, and more.

Every plate is read the same way. **Above**, what this light has meant across traditions, told as symbol and story. **Below**, what it asks of you tonight, in the manner of the ascent: one thing to set down, one thing to receive. Then one passage, always ancient, and one question.

<h2>How to use it</h2>

Open the deck after dark and let your eyes adjust. The firmament opens slowly. Then lift your eyes to any part of the sky. A plate is drawn by chance from the whole deck, and the halo travels to where that light stands tonight, or rests on the horizon if it has set. A mystery beyond the stars rests where you looked. Look at the plate before anything else, for as long as you like. Read opens the book: where the light stands, above, below, the passage, the question. Keep one line, with the heavens as they stood, or let it set. When the same plate opens for you again, the line you kept under it is the first thing in its book. Look again whenever you like.

<h2>What it is not</h2>

The positions are real, computed at your clock's minute from an approximate model good to about a degree, for the center of California unless you ask the deck to read the sky over where you are. That location stays on your device, rounded to about ten kilometers. The signs are the tropical signs of Western astrology. The meanings are the ones people have given those lights for four thousand years, offered as symbols to think with. The plates say what a light has meant and ask what it means tonight. They never say what will happen.
