<Deck deck="wood" />

You hold what you need to see, follow a light into the dark, and come out with a tree, a presence, or a passage. Follow three, and you come out with one of each. The question is never typed. The wood answers questions about the one who walks in.

<h2>The three circles</h2>

Fifty-two cards in three circles, which are also the three positions of every walk. **Elders** are sacred trees across traditions: the ground you stand on, roots, lineage, what holds. **Spirits** are the beings and presences of the wood: what meets you, the otherness of the forest, what moves. **Passages** are what happens to a person among trees: what is happening, the process the wood is working on you. A walk of three lights draws one of each, so a reading has a grammar without needing a separate spread.

The setting is the North American conifer forest. The walk climbs as it darkens: the coast's cedar and fir in mist, the mountains' spruce and aspen, the north woods' fir and birch. The language comes from everywhere. The trees come from here.

<h2>How to use it</h2>

Read the one line at the door and hold a question about yourself. Hold it. You will not be asked to write it down. Not “will it happen” and not “what are they thinking”. The wood answers questions about the one who walks in. It is a mirror, not a telescope.

Walk in and follow a light, and one card turns over. Look at it before anything else, for as long as you like. When you want more, Read opens the book: the card's story, a passage, and, for a Passage card, a question. Keep one line, or leave it on a branch, after the omikuji tied to a pine, and it will never be shown again. Walk again whenever you like.

Choose Three at the door for the full walk. Follow a light, and an Elder turns over on the left wing. Follow a second, and a Spirit turns over on the right. Follow a third, and a Passage opens the center. The book then holds three stories, three passages, and one question. The last line you kept waits under the deck, with a place to say what came of it.

For the table, a choice at the door draws one Passage and turns its question into a prompt for the weekly gathering. Nothing is saved from a table draw.

<h2>Care with living traditions</h2>

Many of these stories belong to nations whose people are alive and practicing. The cards draw only from stories those nations have shared publicly, name the nation every time, and prefer Native-authored sources. No ceremonial objects or restricted ceremonies appear.
